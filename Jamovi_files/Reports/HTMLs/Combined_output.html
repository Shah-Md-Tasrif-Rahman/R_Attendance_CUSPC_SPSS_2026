Statistical Assumptions & Guidelines

Understanding the assumptions behind your analysis helps ensure valid interpretation.

Line Chart Assumptions

  • Continuous Y-variable: The outcome variable should be numeric and measurable.
  • Ordered X-variable: Time points or sequence should have meaningful order.
  • Independence: Observations should be independent unless using appropriate methods for correlated data.

Trend Analysis AssumptionsKey Assumptions:

  • Linearity: The relationship between variables is approximately linear.
  • Independence: Data points are independent of each other.
  • Normality: Residuals are approximately normally distributed.
  • Homoscedasticity: Variance of residuals is constant across fitted values.

Assumption Checks:

  • Potential non-linear relationship detected. Consider polynomial or spline fitting.
  • Residuals appear normally distributed (Shapiro-Wilk p > 0.05).

Clinical Interpretation GuidelinesEffect Size Interpretation:

  • R² ≥ 0.50: Large effect - clinically significant relationship
  • R² = 0.25-0.49: Medium effect - moderate clinical relevance
  • R² = 0.10-0.24: Small effect - limited clinical significance
  • R² < 0.10: Very small effect - minimal clinical importance

Confidence Intervals:

When displayed, confidence intervals show the uncertainty around trend lines. Wider intervals indicate greater uncertainty.

Sample Size Considerations:

Minimum recommendations: 10+ observations for basic trends, 30+ for reliable confidence intervals, 50+ for robust correlation analysis.
